# Industrial deep-learning approaches to LTV prediction (2019 to 2026)

Provenance tags used on every finding:
- **[FT]** = I read the full text (arXiv HTML or PDF, fetched 2026-11-18) and the fact is in the method, experiment or appendix sections.
- **[ABS]** = I read only the abstract and a few passages of the arXiv text.
- **[SNIP]** = the fact comes only from a web-search snippet or from another paper's description of the work; the primary text was not reachable (ACM, Springer, university repositories).

"My arithmetic" marks a ratio I computed from numbers in a paper's table. Nothing else is derived.

Scope caveat that applies throughout: almost every paper below predicts LTV for **new users or ad conversions** from a 1 to 7 day window, over a **1 to 30 day** label horizon. Only four sources work at 180 to 365 days (ZILN, Kuaishou ODMN, Baidu HT-GNN, Douyin TTF), and only one (the Meta/Uber RNN paper) is an existing-user, weekly, rolling-horizon setting like ours.

## 1. ZILN (Wang, Liu, Miao 2019, Google): proposal, metrics, weaknesses

### Takeaway
ZILN is a three-output head (pay probability, lognormal mean, lognormal sigma) whose main contribution beyond the loss is the evaluation protocol (normalized Gini plus decile calibration chart and decile MAPE). Its documented gains are over MSE, on small public datasets with random splits, and later industrial papers repeatedly find its aggregate bias, top-end error and fit to multi-peak spend to be the weak points.

### Cited Findings
- [FT] The head has three pre-activation units: sigmoid for purchase probability p, identity for mu, softplus for sigma; the loss is binary cross-entropy plus the lognormal negative log-likelihood on positive labels. Prediction is p * exp(mu + sigma^2/2). — [Wang et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.07753)
- [FT] The paper targets **new customers**; the label is spend in a fixed horizon after the first purchase, excluding the first purchase. The authors say "an exact number of years for the prediction horizon is preferred to avoid seasonal fluctuations" and that 1, 2 or 3 years is practical. — [Wang et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.07753)
- [FT] Weakness stated by the authors themselves: "Obtaining a good estimate of sigma is crucial as it directly influences the unbiasedness of the mean prediction" because E(X) = exp(mu + sigma^2/2). — [Wang et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.07753)
- [FT] Metrics introduced or advocated: normalized Gini (model Gini divided by label Gini, rank-only, robust to outliers), a "baseline Gini" using first-purchase value as a floor, decile charts (mean prediction against mean label per prediction decile), and decile-level MAPE as the calibration number. AUC or AUC-PR is used for the returning-customer classifier. — [Wang et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.07753)
- [FT] Kaggle Acquire Valued Shoppers (311K customers, top 20 companies, cohort first purchasing 2012-03-01 to 2012-07-01, 12-month label, random 80/20 customer split, DNN with 64 and 32 hidden units): ZILN against MSE gives Spearman +23.9% (linear) and +48.0% (DNN); normalized Gini +28.6% (linear) and +11.4% (DNN); decile MAPE 60.0% lower (linear) and 68.9% lower (DNN). DNN-ZILN beats linear-ZILN by only 2.2% on Spearman. Returning-customer AUC-PR is "comparable" to plain BCE. — [Wang et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.07753)
- [FT] KDD Cup 1998 (about 200K lapsed donors, about 95% zeros, mean of 50 runs): Spearman 0.027 against 0.020, normalized Gini 0.190 against 0.184, decile MAPE 0.176 against 0.210 for ZILN against MSE. — [Wang et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.07753)
- [FT] Kuaishou (180M new users): ZILN's absolute mean bias error (AMBE) grows with horizon: 0.1336 at 30 days, 1.4697 at 90, 4.9021 at 180, 8.6644 at 365, against 0.0423, 0.1263, 0.2460, 0.3123 for ODMN. The authors conclude ZILN "performance on the AMBE is poor". — [Li et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.13358)
- [FT] Multi-modality: Huawei's game-centre data shows non-zero LTV with "multiple peaks on the curve, which is possibly caused by the institutionalized price levels on mobile App stores", and a gamma regressor beat the lognormal and exponential ones there. — [Wu et al. 2023 (CMLTV)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14400)
- [FT] Tencent (about 3M users of a mobile game, predict next-month spend after week 1): ZILN had normalized Gini 0.9581 and top-5% MAPE 0.7500, against 0.9605 and 0.4835 for a plain MLP trained with MSE on log-transformed spend. The authors attribute this to the lognormal assumption not matching their data. — [Cao et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15944)
- [FT] Meituan (three domains, 33M to 248M records, chronological split): ZILN Gini was 0.652, 0.319, 0.419, below MDME (0.794, 0.476, 0.488) and, on domain 1, below plain XGBoost (0.709). — [Zhao et al. 2026 (CC-OR-Net)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10176)
- [FT] Kuaishou 2026, industrial **dwell-time** table (a heavy-tailed target, not LTV): ZILN NMAE 1.094 against 0.696 for log-target MSE and 0.477 for PIT-SUN, and ZILN's signed aggregate "prediction gain" was +0.417 (target 0), the largest over-prediction in the table; its normalized Gini (0.859) was still above the log-MSE rows (0.823) though below PIT-SUN (0.878). — [Zhao et al. 2026 (PIT-SUN)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08202)
- [FT] ZILN cannot represent negative LTV (cost exceeding revenue); AgentLTV lists this as a practical gap of ZILN-family heads. — [Wu et al. 2026 (AgentLTV)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21634)
- [FT] Even with ZILN inside a strong model, bias by value band is systematic: WeChat mini-games reports over-prediction of 344% in the lowest band and under-prediction of 49.8% in the highest band for 3-day value. — [Chen et al. 2025 (GRePO-LTV)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11037)

### Inferences
- ZILN's ranking is usually competitive; its level (calibration, aggregate bias, top-decile error) is where it loses. For our five targets, "expected spend" and "expected hours" are level quantities, so the bake-off should score aggregate bias and decile calibration separately from Gini, or ZILN will look better than it is.
- The Kuaishou AMBE pattern (bias growing from 30 to 365 days) is the single most relevant warning for a 52-week horizon, although it is on a new-user, engagement-type target.
- ZILN's original evidence is against MSE only, on random splits. It was never compared with tree models in the source paper.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that quantifies sigma mis-estimation directly (for example by fixing sigma or sharing it across users). The sensitivity claim rests on the formula and on downstream bias numbers.
- No source tests ZILN on hours or playtime targets.

## 2. Alternatives to ZILN for the output distribution

### Takeaway
Three families exist: (a) bucketed or ordinal heads with an in-bucket regressor (MDME, MDAN, ADSNet, CC-OR-Net), (b) mixtures or routed experts over ZILN-type components (ExpLTV, OptDist, HiLTV, CoGSeq-LTV's ZIMLN), and (c) transform-or-quantize regression heads (CMLTV's log and log2-bin views, PIT-SUN, RQ-Reg, price-point decomposition). Reported gains over ZILN are typically 1 to 6% relative on normalized Gini and much larger on MAE or bias, but no alternative wins consistently when re-implemented by other groups.

### Cited Findings
- [FT] **MDME** (Kuaishou, 2022): splits the label range into sub-distributions and then into roughly equal-frequency buckets; towers classify sub-distribution and bucket (with ordinal-regression towers distilled into them), and a bias tower regresses the normalized position inside the bucket with MSE. They used 2 sub-distributions and say the number of sub-distributions and buckets "have an effect on the final performance, but not very much". — [Li et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.13358)
- [FT] MDME/ODMN against ZILN at 365 days: NRMSE 1.1538 against 1.2103, NMAE 0.5833 against 0.6833, AMBE 0.3123 against 8.6644, Mutual Gini 0.0083 against 0.0558. Online A/B against ZILN in user-growth advertising: ROI +11.9% (7 days), +12.8% (14), +14.7% (30). — [Li et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.13358)
- [FT] MDME does not transfer cleanly. OptDist's re-implementation: MDME normalized Gini 0.4383 against ZILN 0.6338 on Criteo-SSC, 0.6305 against 0.6693 on Kaggle, 0.7277 against 0.7854 on a 4.5M-sample fund platform; the authors call it "unstable across the datasets" and blame accumulated error across the sub-distribution, bucket and in-bucket stages. ExpLTV found MDME "not applicable in our scenario" (Tencent games). — [Weng et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08585); [Zhang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12729)
- [FT] **ExpLTV** (Tencent games, CIKM 2023): a whale detector is trained on a soft label p = 1 - exp(-LTV/R) and acts as the gate mixing two ZILN experts (whale and low spender), with a shared pay-probability estimator. Three games (135K to 293K users, whales 0.46% to 1.21%, purchase rate 7.5% to 14.4%), 7-day LTV, split in time (40 days train, 3 validate, 3 test). Normalized Gini improved 1.5%, 3.8% and 6.2% relative to the best baseline. Removing the experts cut Gini on game A from 0.739 to 0.577. — [Zhang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12729)
- [FT] **OptDist** (Tencent FiT, CIKM 2024): several ZILN-type sub-distribution networks plus a Gumbel-softmax selector trained with pseudo-labels from each sub-network's loss. Against ZILN: MAE 15.784 against 20.880 and normalized Gini 0.6437 against 0.6338 (Criteo-SSC); 70.929 against 72.528 and 0.6814 against 0.6693 (Kaggle); 0.322 against 0.389 and 0.8283 against 0.7854 (industrial). Best number of sub-distributions was 4 or 5. Online ROI lifts of +6.5% to +21.9% across four campaigns at 7 to 30 days. All offline splits were random 7:1:2. — [Weng et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08585)
- [FT] **MDAN** (Tencent, PAKDD 2024), as described and re-run by OptDist: multi-class bucket classifier plus multi-channel embeddings fused by the class probabilities. Normalized Gini 0.6209, 0.6648, 0.7629 on the three OptDist datasets, below ZILN on all three. [SNIP] The MDAN paper itself claims deployment in several Tencent applications; I could not read its numbers. — [Weng et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08585); [Springer listing](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-97-2259-4_31)
- [FT] **CMLTV** (Huawei, 2023): three heads trained jointly on one backbone (a gamma-distribution head, an MSE head on log(1+y), and a classification head over bins floor(log2(1+y))), plus contrastive losses, fused at inference. 30-day LTV, 642K positive and 30.1M negative samples, test on the last day. RMSE 240.16 against 242.56 (ZILN) and 243.71 (MDME); MAE 6.889 against 8.090 and 7.969; AUC 0.8629 against 0.8601 and 0.8482. XGBoost had the lowest MAE of all (4.681). A 70-day A/B test reported +32.26% payment amount for +9% inference latency. — [Wu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14400)
- [FT] In CMLTV, swapping the backbone (MLP, DCN, AutoInt) moved ZILN's RMSE only between 242.14 and 242.56, less than the head change did. — [Wu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14400)
- [FT] **ADSNet** (Tencent Ads, KDD 2024): replaces ZILN with an ordinal-classification amount head (K-1 binary "exceeds threshold" classifiers, expected value from segment means). Backbone alone against ZILN: average AUC 0.822 against 0.794, average Gini 0.820 against 0.794. — [Wang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10517)
- [FT] **CC-OR-Net** (Meituan, WWW 2026): cascaded ordinal classification over 4 buckets (zero plus three quantile buckets) so that ordering is structural, an in-bucket residual regressor, and a high-value augmentation module. Gini 0.803, 0.490, 0.501 against the best baseline per domain 0.794 (MDME), 0.487 (MMoE with focal loss), 0.488 (MDME). AMBE 4.85, 0.90, 21.18 against ZILN 12.81, 5.73, 70.66. The augmentation module cut AMBE by 25.0%. Recall@5000 of whales 38.1% against 36.5% (ExpLTV) and 34.2% (OptDist). Models have about 1M to 2M parameters. — [Zhao et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10176)
- [FT] **Price-point decomposition (CALTV)** (TapTap, 2025): predict capped transaction counts at each "significant price" by classification, then sum count times price. 24-hour LTV, about 30M conversions over 12 months. Area under Lorenz curve 0.902 against 0.886 (ZILN) and 0.888 (ExpLTV); group-bias variance on the top 80% 0.0469 against 0.0824 and 0.1017, but worse overall (0.7353 against 0.4033) because of over-prediction in the lowest two deciles. Online: "over 20%" higher 24-hour ROI than ZILN. — [Li et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08281)
- [FT] **Zero-inflated mixture lognormal** heads: CoGSeq-LTV uses K=3 lognormal components plus a zero mass and an MAE-aligned auxiliary loss; removing the mixture head raised 30-day e-commerce MAE from 2199 to 2459 and removing the MAE-aligned term raised it to 2615. [SNIP] HiLTV (SIGIR 2025, online games) uses a "ZIMoL" loss for zero, unimodal and multimodal spending. — [Su et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01817); [Wu et al. 2026, reference list and baseline description](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21634)
- [FT] **Transform-and-recover** (PIT-SUN, Kuaishou 2026): log, sqrt or Box-Cox targets with direct inversion estimate T^-1(E[T(Y)|x]) and not E[Y|x] ("retransformation bias"); the method learns a multiplicative recovery term. On a public transaction-amount set, NMAE 0.207 against 0.221 (ZILN) and 0.346 (MDME). — [Zhao et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08202)
- [FT] **Residual quantization** (RQ-Reg, CIKM 2026): autoregressively predicts coarse-to-fine quantization codes of the target. On Kaggle: MAE 59.340 against 70.929 (OptDist) and 72.528 (ZILN), normalized Gini 0.7235 against 0.6814 and 0.6693. On Criteo-SSC it loses on Gini (0.6141 against 0.6437 OptDist). — [Cui et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23012)
- [FT] **Classification-as-regression (histogram, HL-Gauss)**: the evidence is from reinforcement learning value functions, where categorical cross-entropy scaled better than MSE under noisy, non-stationary targets. It is not an LTV result. — [Farebrother et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03950)
- [FT] **Tweedie and gamma**: CC-OR-Net dismisses "specific statistical forms (e.g., ZILN, Tweedie)" as too rigid, without a head-to-head test. The only deep head-to-head on gamma against lognormal that I read is CMLTV's (gamma better on its multi-peak data, shown in a figure, no table). — [Zhao et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10176); [Wu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14400)
- [FT] **Automated search** (AgentLTV, 2026): LLM agents search over pipeline programs; the best discovered model combined temporal-convolution and attention branches with a negative-aware output and calibration. Reported normalized Gini 0.963 against 0.813 for HiLTV and 0.775 for OptDist on a proprietary set of about 400K samples per period. — [Wu et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21634)

### Inferences
- Rank order of evidence quality: CC-OR-Net (three domains, up to 248M records, chronological split, eleven baselines) and OptDist (three datasets, public code-style baselines) are the most credible; both agree that a plain ZILN head is beatable and that MDME is strong on bias but unstable on ranking.
- The gains concentrate in calibration and tail error, the dimensions where ZILN is weak. A head swap inside candidate C is nearly free at scoring time (CC-OR-Net and CMLTV add under 10% latency and about 1M to 2M parameters), so it does not threaten the 30-minute constraint. Rough arithmetic: 100M users in 30 minutes is about 56K users per second, which batch GPU inference of a small GRU handles regardless of head type.
- Candidates worth adding as head ablations in C (and, where expressible, in the LightGBM arm): (1) ordinal buckets plus in-bucket residual (CC-OR-Net or ADSNet style, simpler than MDME); (2) zero-inflated mixture of 3 lognormals; (3) log2-bin classification head. A two-expert whale-routed head (ExpLTV) is a fourth, cheaper variant of (2).
- AgentLTV's and HT-GNN's margins (Gini jumps of 0.15 or more over all prior methods) are out of line with every other paper and come from small or unusual test sets; I would not weight them.
- Price-point decomposition fits fixed IAP price ladders; it does not obviously fit a 52-week cumulative spend with many purchases, and has no analogue for hours.

### Gaps
- No paper tests any of these heads on a 52-week, existing-user target. All gains are for new users or conversions at 1 to 30 days, except Kuaishou (365 days, new users).
- No deep-network Tweedie or zero-inflated gamma head is benchmarked against ZILN in a table in anything I could read.
- Quantile (pinball) heads: only the ZILN paper discusses them (as losing the mean), no industrial LTV benchmark found.
- MDAN, HiLTV, USE-LTV, PTMSN (OPPO) and MEAN (2025) primary texts were unreachable; their own numbers are missing here.

## 3. Multi-horizon consistency and long-horizon prediction

### Takeaway
Only Kuaishou's ODMN is explicitly built to keep LTV at 30, 90, 180 and 365 days ordered, and it does so softly (a non-negative-weight "Mono Unit" plus a hinge penalty), not by construction. Two other sources reach 360 to 365 days (Baidu HT-GNN and Douyin TTF), and one predicts rolling 4, 13 and 26 week value for existing users (Meta Quest). Nothing targets weekly resolution over 52 weeks.

### Cited Findings
- [FT] **ODMN**: one MDME head per horizon on a shared bottom; the upstream horizon's normalized bucket distribution passes (with stop-gradient) through an MLP with non-negative weights and is added to the downstream horizon's logits; a calibration loss penalizes max(y_t - y_{t+1}, 0). Features are 7 days of day-level behaviour sequences for 180M new users, plus periodic and seasonal features. — [Li et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.13358)
- [FT] ODMN ablation at 365 days: plain shared-bottom NRMSE 1.2163 and AMBE 0.6475; with Mono Unit 1.1995 and 0.5496; with calibration loss 1.1862 and 0.4155; both 1.1538 and 0.3123. The authors note "conventional multi-task learning has limited improvement" and that long horizons gain more than short ones from the ordering. — [Li et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.13358)
- [FT] Kuaishou scores the **full user base daily** in batch and caches results for online lookup. — [Li et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.13358)
- [FT] ExpLTV's authors describe the Kuaishou target as a DAU-type (activity) LTV, not spend. — [Zhang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12729)
- [FT] **HT-GNN** (Baidu Ads, 2026): predicts both lifetime (LT, activity) and LTV at 30, 180 and 365 days with a mixture-of-experts and per-horizon towers; 15M users, September 2023 to March 2025, 35 behaviour-sequence types, random 9:1 split. At 365 days: LT NRMSE 0.8083 against 0.9987 (MDME) and 1.3865 (ZILN). On LTV-180 its Gini (0.6843) is below ExpLTV (0.7491) and XGBoost (0.7363), despite the abstract's "across all metrics" claim. — [Zhao et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13013)
- [FT] **TTF** (Douyin, 2025): forecasts the **channel-level** LTV curve for days 30 to 360 from the first 30 days ("short-input long-output"), stacking curves of earlier cohorts in a trapezoid so the model sees longer histories; calendar (day, week, month, year) and one-hot holiday covariates. Best point-wise MAPE 13.5% and aggregate MAPE 9.2%, against 16.1% and 11.1% for the statistical model; deployed with MAPE reductions of 4.3% and 3.2% against the previous online model. — [Wan et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.17639)
- [FT] **Rolling LTV with RNNs** (Meta Quest, existing users): user-level revenue at 1, 4, 13 and 26 weeks ahead from series up to 4 years long with 100+ features including seasonal ones. Adjusted SMAPE: BTYD 53.6%, 70.5%, 78.3%; LightGBM 30.4%, 53.7%, 61.1%; dilated RNN 22.9%, 39.8%, 51.2% at 4, 13, 26 weeks. — [Chen et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20295)
- [FT] **Pareto optimization across horizons** (WeChat mini-games, KDD 2025): treats 3, 7 and 30 day value as conflicting tasks and descends a non-dominated gradient. Removing it raised average NMAE from 0.188 to 0.221 and cut the online GMV gain from 8.4% to 0.11%. — [Chen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11037)
- [FT] CoGSeq-LTV (7, 14, 30, 45 days) records that cumulative labels are non-decreasing but leaves "hard monotonic output constraints as a natural extension". — [Su et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01817)
- [FT] Revolut's foundation model frames LTV as a binary "positive gross profit" task with horizons of "6 months or more" from a couple of weeks of history. — [Ostroukhov et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08649)
- [SNIP] SHORE (2025) claimed 60-day game LTV from 7 days with an order-preserving classification loss plus Huber loss; the arXiv record now reads "withdrawn" (I confirmed the withdrawal notice on arXiv). — [arXiv 2506.10487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10487)

### Inferences
- Candidate C's 52 weekly activity sigmoids already give expected active weeks at any horizon as a sum of non-negative terms, so cumulative activity is monotone by construction. That is stronger than ODMN's soft constraint. The gap is on hours and spend, where C has a single 52-week ZILN head.
- The transferable idea is to add intermediate cumulative horizons (for example 4, 13, 26, 52 weeks) for hours and spend as auxiliary tasks, parameterized as non-negative increments so ordering holds by construction. ODMN's ablation suggests the benefit is largest at the longest horizon (AMBE roughly halved at 365 days), and short-horizon labels mature sooner, which helps freshness.
- TTF's trapezoid idea (borrow longer histories from older cohorts) is for aggregate curves; its user-level analogue is simply training on older anchor dates, which the plan already implies.
- Horizon-task conflict is real (WeChat); if intermediate horizons are added, check that the 52-week metrics do not degrade.

### Gaps
- No paper enforces monotonic multi-horizon outputs by construction for spend, and none reports weekly-resolution outputs to one year.
- ODMN's ordering result is on an activity-type label; no evidence on whether it helps heavy-tailed spend at one year.
- The Meta paper does not state its loss, user count or split protocol in the text I read, and reports only one metric.

## 4. Architectures: contrastive, graph, sequence encoders, foundation models, LLMs

### Takeaway
I found no clean evidence that Transformers beat GRU or LSTM encoders for LTV on behaviour sequences; where both appear, differences are small or mixed, and combining sequence with aggregate features matters more. Graph and contrastive modules report large gains but target new-user sparsity. Pretrained user foundation models show modest LTV gains (single-digit percent) and LLM-based methods are cold-start tools.

### Cited Findings
- [FT] **Sequence encoders compared directly** (WeChat mini-games, 3.73M paying-user samples; average over 3, 7, 30 day value): LSTM NMAE 0.319 and normalized Gini 0.940; Informer (Transformer) 0.325 and 0.944; TCN 0.333 and 0.940; ARIMA 0.319 and 0.944. LTV-specific models were better: ZILN 0.308 and 0.945, ODMN 0.263 and 0.961, ADSNet 0.219 and 0.967, GRePO-LTV 0.188 and 0.982. — [Chen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11037)
- [FT] **RNN against GBDT for existing users**: the dilated-RNN cell (a gated cell with separate output and control states and a delayed-state input) beat LightGBM by 7.5 to 13.9 points of adjusted SMAPE at 4 to 26 weeks on Meta Quest. At Uber (new-user LTV to day 90), a 2-cell LSTM without embeddings was worse than XGBoost (RMSE 2,830 against 2,292) and only edged it with city embeddings (2,271). — [Chen et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20295)
- [FT] **Sequence plus aggregates** (casual freemium game churn): random forest AUC 0.8405, MLP on aggregates 0.8559, LSTM on sequences 0.8592, LSTM combined with aggregated features 0.8729 to 0.8741. — [Kristensen and Burelli 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.03184)
- [FT] **CNN on raw sequences** (Age of Ishtaria): CNN and MLP had nearly identical accuracy (prediction NRMSE 1.05 and 1.12), with the CNN cheaper because it needs no feature engineering. — [Chen et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12799)
- [FT] **Contrastive multi-view (CMLTV)**: gains quoted in section 2; the authors found a direct pair-wise contrast mechanism had a negative effect and used a hybrid scheme; contrastive learning accounts for most of the added training cost. — [Wu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14400)
- [FT] **Graph models**: GRePO-LTV builds user-user and game-game graphs from shared interactions to pretrain embeddings; removing them raised average NMAE from 0.188 to 0.208 and cut online GMV gain from 8.4% to 2.4%. CoGSeq-LTV's batch-induced user-item graph was its most important component (30-day e-commerce MAE 3138 without it, 2199 with). HT-GNN's hypergraph over demographic segments beat GCN, GraphSAGE and GAT variants. — [Chen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11037); [Su et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01817); [Zhao et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13013)
- [FT] **TSUR** (Tencent, KDD 2021), as described by AgentLTV: multi-channel discrete wavelet transform with GRU, attention and a graph attention network. In AgentLTV's benchmark TSUR had the best Spearman among prior methods (0.680 against 0.515 for OptDist). — [Wu et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21634)
- [FT] **perCLTV** (NetEase, TOIS 2023) appears as a baseline in CoGSeq-LTV and is weak there: 30-day e-commerce MAE 4912 against 2764 (ODMN) and 2280 (MDAN). [SNIP] A survey describes it as gated sequential multi-task learning over churn and payment for online games. — [Su et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01817); [survey snippet](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.08502)
- [SNIP] MarfNet (Tencent IEG, WSDM 2023) routes and fuses features with explicit missing-feature awareness for mobile-game ad LTV; MDLUR (KDD 2023) is a multi-datasource LTV user representation. No numbers read. — [DBLP MarfNet](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/wsdm/YangJWZ23); [DBLP MDLUR](https://dblp.uni-trier.de/rec/conf/kdd/YunKK23.html)
- [FT] **Foundation models**: Revolut's PRAGMA (masked-modelling Transformer, 10M to 1B parameters) changes LTV PR-AUC by -1.2%, +0.3% and +1.8% (small, medium, large) against the task-specific baseline, far less than its gains on credit scoring (+130.2% PR-AUC for large); the authors call LTV gains "more modest" and say the small model is an efficiency sweet spot for LTV. — [Ostroukhov et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08649)
- [ABS] Other pretrained sequence encoders used for LTV-type downstream tasks: CASPR (Microsoft, Transformer over customer activity sequences with masked-entity pretraining, embeddings used alone or alongside RFM features); a GRU autoencoder of the full event sequence placed second in RecSys Challenge 2025 on universal behavioural profiles; PinnerFormer (Pinterest) trains a Transformer with a "dense all-action" loss to predict long-term future engagement so that once-daily batch embeddings approach real-time ones. — [Kulkarni et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09174); [Klenitskiy et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07748); [Pancha et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.04507)
- [FT] **LLM-based**: Kuaishou's ScaleToT distils LLM tree-of-thought reasoning over static profiles into a small encoder for low-activity users; online LT30 +6.738% with LLM inference on only 7.32% of the population. Meituan's SemRaD uses LLM-built semantic profiles and hindsight distillation for new users: LTV Gini 0.2847 to 0.2902 (+1.9%). — [Ma et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24605); [Le et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17070)

### Inferences
- The plan's choice to run both GRU and Transformer is consistent with the literature: nothing I read justifies dropping the GRU, and the Informer result suggests a generic Transformer may not win. The dilated-RNN variant from the Meta paper is the one extra sequence encoder with direct evidence in a setting like ours (existing users, weekly, up to 26 weeks), and it is cheap to try as a cell swap.
- Feeding aggregate (RFM and trailing-window) features alongside the sequence into C is supported by two independent sources and costs nothing at scoring time.
- Graph, cross-domain and LLM methods solve sparsity for users with little history. With 104 weeks of own history per existing user and no social graph in the stated data, their expected value is low and their scoring cost at 100M users is high. I would not add them as candidates.
- Self-supervised pretraining (already optional in C) has weak LTV-specific support: PRAGMA's LTV gains are near zero for small models. Treat masked-week pretraining as a low-priority ablation.

### Gaps
- No paper compares GRU against Transformer on long (about 100-step) per-user sequences for a one-year LTV target.
- I found no NetEase or Tencent paper using a game social graph for LTV whose text I could read; TSUR and perCLTV details are second-hand.
- USE-LTV (Baidu, CIKM 2025, Transformer over "uncertain" behaviour sequences) is known only from citations.

## 5. Game industry: whales, churn-and-return, seasons

### Takeaway
Game papers agree on extreme concentration (about 2% of players giving up to 50% of revenue), on deep models beating BTYD mainly at the top end, and on systematic under-prediction of the top decile. Evidence on churn-and-return is limited to Yokozuna's work on "false churners", and I found no peer-reviewed paper on season or battle-pass effects in LTV models.

### Cited Findings
- [FT] **Chen et al. 2018** (Yokozuna Data, Age of Ishtaria, about 32 months of data): whales are "around 2% of all players" and "may provide up to 50% of the total revenue". MLP and CNN beat Pareto/NBD and MBG/CNBD-k: prediction RMSLE 1.82 and 1.84 against 9.35 (Pareto/NBD) and 3.46 (MBG/CNBD-k); percent error 5.82% and 5.72% against 8.96% and 7.96%. For the top 20% of spenders, deep models' errors were "roughly half as large as in parametric models". Parametric models often predicted no purchases for players who kept spending. Sample is small: 2,505 paying users who churned in the evaluation year. (Table values parsed from PDF text; column alignment checked by hand.) — [Chen et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12799)
- [FT] Churn definition there: 9 days of inactivity, chosen because players wrongly tagged as churners who later returned contributed "far less than 1%" of monthly revenue. — [Chen et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12799)
- [FT] **Churn-and-return** (same studio and game, about 6,000 whales): players are classed as false churners (never really left), resurrected (returned after a long gap), purchase-resurrected, and "zombies" (extremely sporadic). Dropping certain combinations of zombies and false churners from the training sample improved all churn and purchase-churn models. The inactivity threshold was 9 days for whales and 13 for all payers, and the share of false churners rises with longer data windows. — [del Río et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.03947)
- [ABS] The same group uses an LSTM for per-player LTV alongside survival ensembles for lifetime, level and playtime, to profile players. — [del Río et al. 2019b](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.03870)
- [FT] **Burelli 2022 review** (Tactile Games): free-to-play has "no visible churn event"; whether a customer can return after inactivity determines the choice of churn formulation; most players never purchase, so a model must identify future payers before their first purchase. It reports Sifa et al.'s framing of LTV as three tasks (will the player ever purchase, number of purchases, value per purchase), with tree models best for the classification task and resampling valuable because payers are rare. — [Burelli 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.12619)
- [SNIP] **Sifa et al. 2018** (HICSS): SMOTE augmentation improved prediction for premium and high-value users, especially combined with deep networks. [FT via Chen 2018] It predicted one-year cumulative spend from the first seven days. — [Fraunhofer record](https://publica.fraunhofer.de/handle/publica/401251); [Chen et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.12799)
- [FT] **Tencent games (ExpLTV)**: whale share 0.46% to 1.21% of users; purchase rate 7.5% to 14.4%; the authors argue for short labels (7 days) "due to the frequent changes in app updates, market and user base". Whale-detection Recall@500 rose to 0.333, 0.340, 0.175 from the best baselines' 0.299, 0.302, 0.160. — [Zhang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12729)
- [FT] **Top-end under-prediction is universal**: TapTap reports all three models (ZILN, ExpLTV, CALTV) "underestimated the top first-tier samples and overestimated the lowest two deciles"; WeChat reports the same sign pattern by value band. — [Li et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08281); [Chen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11037)
- [FT] **Seasonality in inputs**: Kuaishou adds periodic, seasonal and holiday features because LTV is "sensitive to seasonal or abnormal events"; Douyin uses calendar and holiday covariates over the forecast horizon; the Meta/Uber paper models calendar-date ("contemporaneous") effects such as events and outages jointly with cohort and age effects, and says BTYD cannot easily take seasonality. — [Li et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.13358); [Wan et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.17639); [Chen et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20295)
- [SNIP] An Aalto master's thesis (Tapper, 2022; freemium mobile game, 360-day LTV) found a deep MLP best among MLP, random forest, gradient boosting and linear regression, and that adding seasonal features did not improve most models. — [Aaltodoc](https://aaltodoc.aalto.fi/items/8b46f0ea-5adc-48ef-a6f4-444264679673/full)

### Inferences
- The return-after-lapse population is known to distort churn-style training labels. For candidate C this argues against any hard "churned" state and for per-week activity targets (as planned), and it is a point in favour of comparing C with the HMM candidate specifically on lapsed-then-returned users.
- Season launches are known in advance. Every source that handles calendar effects does so by feeding future calendar covariates to the model; for C this means season-calendar features on the 52 output steps (known-future covariates), not just on the 104 input weeks. No paper tests this for game seasons, so it is an untested but cheap addition.
- A whale auxiliary task (soft label from spend, ExpLTV style) and top-k capture metrics are the game-specific additions with direct evidence.

### Gaps
- I found no paper on battle-pass or season-launch effects on LTV or on returning-player prediction in live-service games. Searches returned vendor blogs only. This is the largest gap relative to the product described.
- Sifa et al. full texts and perCLTV were unreachable; no numbers from them.
- No game paper studies hours or playtime as an LTV-style zero-inflated target at a long horizon.

## 6. Training tricks widely reported as helpful

### Takeaway
The recurring, evidenced tricks are: joint pay-probability and amount learning on a shared trunk, auxiliary ordinal or whale tasks, log-space or bucketed targets with an explicit correction back to the mean, error-aligned auxiliary losses and post-hoc calibration, and targeted handling of the high-value tail. Payer re-weighting and resampling are used but criticized as distribution-dependent.

### Cited Findings
- [FT] **Shared trunk for pay probability and amount**: ZILN's classification quality matched a stand-alone BCE model, so the joint head costs nothing on the classifier. OptDist notes two-stage models can beat multi-task MSE on positive-only ranking because of the "seesaw phenomenon". — [Wang et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.07753); [Weng et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08585)
- [FT] **Ordinal auxiliary tasks with distillation**: in MDME, adding ordinal-regression towers distilled into the classifiers lowered 365-day NRMSE from 1.4006 to 1.2119 and AMBE from 5.34 to 0.56 (with the bias tower). In CC-OR-Net, cascaded ordinal classification raised Gini from 0.671 to 0.798 on domain 1. — [Li et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.13358); [Zhao et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10176)
- [FT] **Whale detection as auxiliary task and gate**: see ExpLTV in sections 2 and 5. — [Zhang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12729)
- [FT] **Log transform**: an MLP with MSE on log spend beat ZILN on Gini and top-5% MAPE in one Tencent game; but direct inversion of a log target is biased for the mean, and PIT-SUN's industrial dwell-time table (not LTV) shows log-target MSE under-predicting in aggregate (signed prediction gain -0.193, target 0) while ZILN over-predicts (+0.417). — [Cao et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15944); [Zhao et al. 2026 (PIT-SUN)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08202)
- [FT] **Monte Carlo dropout averaging**: top-5% MAPE fell from 0.4835 to 0.1858 (MLP) and 0.4226 to 0.2003 (DCNv2) with almost unchanged Gini, and yields a per-user confidence measure. It multiplies inference passes. — [Cao et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15944)
- [FT] **Error-aligned auxiliary loss and calibrated inference**: CoGSeq-LTV adds an MAE-aligned point loss to the likelihood and tunes a zero-aware decision rule on validation data; AgentLTV's best pipeline included an explicit calibration step and the authors recommend bucket-level ranking and calibration diagnostics before deployment. — [Su et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01817); [Wu et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21634)
- [FT] **High-value augmentation**: a separate module with a relative-error loss on top-bucket users cut AMBE 25.0% and top-bucket NRMSE 13.9% for a small latency cost (0.64 to 0.79 ms in their table). — [Zhao et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10176)
- [FT] **Re-weighting and resampling**: Kuaishou argues re-weighting depends on the label prior and must be redone when the distribution shifts, and that over-sampling over-fits the tail; the Meta paper sub-samples zero-transaction periods and adds "weeks since last step" and week-of-year features to compensate for uneven steps. [SNIP] Sifa et al. report SMOTE helps for high-value users. — [Li et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.13358); [Chen et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20295); [Fraunhofer record](https://publica.fraunhofer.de/handle/publica/401251)
- [FT] **Multi-task backbone choice is secondary**: ODMN uses a plain shared bottom and says MMoE or PLE can be substituted; CMLTV's backbone swap changed little. — [Li et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.13358); [Wu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14400)
- [FT] **Distillation from privileged or cross-domain signals**: SemRaD distils post-conversion ("hindsight") information into the deployed student; ADSNet transfers from external purchase data with a gain-evaluation filter to reject harmful samples (online LTV +3.47%, GMV +3.89%). Both are new-user settings. — [Le et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17070); [Wang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10517)
- [FT] **Incremental daily fine-tuning** is the deployment norm in ad settings (TapTap: train on 10 months, then fine-tune each day and predict the next). — [Li et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08281)

### Inferences
- For C, the low-cost, evidence-backed additions are: an ordinal or whale auxiliary head, an MAE- or bias-aligned auxiliary term, and a post-hoc calibration layer (per-decile or isotonic) fitted on a held-out later period. All are scoring-time neutral.
- MC dropout's top-end gain is large but multiplies scoring cost by the number of passes; at 100M users weekly it only fits if the pass count is small or it is applied to a pre-filtered high-value subset.
- Sub-sampling zero weeks (Meta) could shrink sequence length for sparse users and help both training and the scoring budget, at the cost of irregular steps that need gap features.

### Gaps
- No controlled study isolates payer sample-weighting inside a deep LTV model.
- Calibration layers are mentioned but never ablated with numbers in what I read.

## 7. Evaluation practice

### Takeaway
The de facto standard set is normalized Gini (ranking), AUC for the payer classifier, a normalized error (NMAE or NRMSE or MAE), and a bias or calibration measure (AMBE, decile MAPE or decile bias). Top-k whale recall and positive-only variants are common additions. Splits are random in a surprising share of papers; the stronger industrial papers split in time.

### Cited Findings
- [FT] **Ranking**: normalized Gini is used by ZILN, ExpLTV, OptDist, ADSNet, GRePO-LTV, HT-GNN, CC-OR-Net, MC-dropout and AgentLTV; Spearman is the usual companion (ZILN, OptDist, CMLTV, CC-OR-Net). OptDist and CC-OR-Net also report both on positive samples only. — [Wang et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.07753); [Weng et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08585); [Zhao et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10176)
- [FT] **Criticism of Gini**: Kuaishou says the Gini coefficient "is not a good choice to reflect the performance difference of fitting imbalanced distribution" and proposes Mutual Gini (area between the Lorenz curves of predicted and true LTV). CC-OR-Net adds that a high global Gini "can mask deficiencies in critical high-value segments". — [Li et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.13358); [Zhao et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10176)
- [FT] **Level and bias**: NRMSE, NMAE and AMBE (Kuaishou, CC-OR-Net, HT-GNN); decile MAPE (ZILN); variance of decile biases around their median (TapTap); bias by true-value band (WeChat); adjusted SMAPE with a floor on small actuals for user-level rolling LTV (Meta); point-wise and aggregate MAPE for cohort curves (Douyin); top-5% MAPE because MAPE is undefined on zeros (Tencent). — [Li et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08281); [Chen et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20295); [Cao et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15944)
- [FT] **Classifier**: AUC (ExpLTV, CMLTV, ADSNet, GRePO-LTV, HT-GNN), AUC-PR (ZILN), F1 for zero against non-zero (CC-OR-Net). — [Zhang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12729); [Wu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14400)
- [FT] **Top-k capture**: Recall@K of whales (ExpLTV: K = 500 to 5000; CC-OR-Net: Recall@5000), top-5% hit rate (Tencent, about 36% for all models), area under the Lorenz curve (TapTap), and a stratified accuracy over zero, low and high strata (CC-OR-Net's SVA). — [Zhang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12729); [Zhao et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10176)
- [FT] **Online metrics**: ROI at 7, 14, 30 days (Kuaishou, OptDist), GMV or payment amount (GRePO-LTV, CMLTV, ADSNet), activity LT30 (ScaleToT). — [Li et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.13358); [Weng et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08585)
- [FT] **Time-based splits**: ExpLTV 40 days train, 3 validate, 3 test; ADSNet 70, 10, 10 days; CMLTV tests on the last day; CC-OR-Net splits chronologically with 40% test; TapTap uses rolling daily fine-tune and next-day evaluation over 2 months; CoGSeq-LTV adds a later out-of-time window and documents temporal cutoffs against feature-label leakage. — [Zhang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12729); [Wang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10517); [Su et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01817)
- [FT] **Random splits**: ZILN (80/20 within a cohort), OptDist (7:1:2), HT-GNN (9:1, test stratified by realized lifetime), GRePO-LTV (7:2:1, not stated as chronological). — [Weng et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08585); [Zhao et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13013)
- [FT] **Tree baselines are often competitive when included**: XGBoost had the best MAE in CMLTV; two-stage XGBoost reached Gini 0.774 on CC-OR-Net's domain 1 against 0.803 for the proposed model and 0.652 for ZILN; XGBoost beat a feature-only LSTM at Uber. Many papers (ZILN, ExpLTV, OptDist, ODMN) include no tree baseline at all. — [Wu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14400); [Zhao et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10176); [Chen et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20295)

### Inferences
- A bake-off scorecard consistent with this literature: normalized Gini and Spearman (all users and positives only), AUC for the two probability targets, NMAE, aggregate bias (AMBE or sum-predicted over sum-actual), decile calibration chart with decile MAPE, top-k (for example top 1% and 5%) capture of spend, all computed on a forward-in-time holdout. Reporting bias by true-value band and by segment (lapsed, new, steady) would expose the pattern every game paper sees.
- Because several headline results come from random splits, expect smaller gains under the plan's temporal split than the papers report.
- The frequency with which gradient-boosted trees are omitted or win on MAE supports keeping candidate A as a serious contender and running the ZILN-alternative heads there too where the library allows.

### Gaps
- No paper evaluates probabilistic calibration of the full predictive distribution (for example CRPS or interval coverage) for LTV; evaluation is on means and ranks.
- None of the papers reports scoring throughput for a full user base in users per second; CC-OR-Net gives per-unit latency and memory only, and Kuaishou states daily full-volume batch scoring without timings.
